# 41 — Physical models: Hammerwork Grand and Skinwork Kit

Two machines synthesize their sound by simulating the instrument's
mechanics, sample by sample:

- `grand` (**Hammerwork Grand**) is a piano: per key, felt hammers strike
  stiff strings that drive a soundboard through a moving bridge.
- `kit` (**Skinwork Kit**) is a drum kit: sticks and beaters strike
  membranes and cymbals.

They share a small layer of physical-modeling kernels,
`kernels/10-physical/`. **Status:** built 2026-10-16 on branch
`claude/physical-piano-drums`.

**Clean-room.** Everything here comes from the published literature
cited inline and from measurements of CC0 recordings. Nothing comes from
another physical-modeling codebase or its data. The scale curves,
hammer and kit constants are literature values or our own estimates, and
each is marked below.

Code:
- `kernels/10-physical/modal.fy`: exact modal step, T60 → decay rate,
  implicit power-law contact.
- `kernels/10-physical/bessel.fy`: the circular membrane's first 40
  modes, generated by `tools/fit/bessel.py`.
- `kernels/06-voices/piano.fy` and `machines/grand/`: the piano.
- `kernels/06-voices/kit.fy` and `machines/kit/`: the kit.

## The building blocks (`kernels/10-physical`)

| word | what it is | source |
|---|---|---|
| `zoh-coefs` `modal-step` | one damped mode advanced exactly under a force held for the sample: the undamped closed form plus a decay factor. It is stable at any frequency below Nyquist and keeps the asked-for decay at any pitch. | modal synthesis, Adrien 1991 |
| `t60>sigma` | the decay rate that falls 60 dB in T60 | — |
| `contact-solve` | a striker pressed into a body with `F = K e^p (1 + α ė)`, solved implicitly by four Newton steps on the scalar compliance equation `e = e_free − D F`. It is stable for treble-hammer stiffness, where an explicit force blows up. | Hall 1986, Stulov 1995 (felt); Hunt & Crossley 1975 (damping); Bilbao 2009 ch. 4 |
| `memb-ratio` `memb-m` `memb-shape-at` | circular membrane modes: Bessel-zero ratios j_mn/j_01, nodal diameters, and the radial shape J_m(j_mn r/a) at any radius | Rayleigh; Fletcher & Rossing §3.6 |

A body is a bank of these modes in fixed-size state arrays, unrolled with
`times`. Each mode's coefficients are built at note-on, so the render only
multiplies and adds. The words take physical units (metres, newtons,
kilograms), so a striker's character comes from its mass and felt law,
not from a filter.

Other techniques the two voices use, each a candidate for its own kernel
once a third machine needs it:

- **Moving-bridge coupling.** A modal string whose end is shaken feels
  `−c_n b̈` with `c_n = 2(−1)^(n+1)/(nπ)`, and pushes on the bridge with
  `Σ (−1)^(n+1) nπT/L q_n`. This is a textbook derivation from the string's
  kinetic energy.
- **Resistive dense-mode board.** Above its first modes a board acts like
  a resistance at the bridge (an infinite plate's mobility `1/(8√(D m''))`,
  Cremer). It is high-passed so the board stays stiff at low frequency.
- **First-order tension modulation.** Each mode's step rotation is bent
  by the head's strain energy. The step stays a rotation, so it stays
  stable, where an explicit stiffness force diverges on the upper modes
  (Berger's approximation; Bilbao 2009 ch. 13).
- **Bounded cubic mode coupling.** One shared coordinate carries a
  quartic potential, so the coupling only moves energy between modes.
  Its stiffness is capped so the explicit step stays stable.
- **Diffuse-field energy store.** The modes too dense to simulate are one
  energy reservoir fed by the discrete modes and radiated as band noise:
  statistical energy analysis (Lyon 1975).
- **Lumped collision rattle.** A mass on a spring that collides with a
  body through `contact-solve`. Its collision force, times noise, drives a
  wide resonant bank. This is the snare wires.

## Hammerwork Grand

Per voice (one key), from `piano.fy`:

| part | model |
|---|---|
| strings | Up to 3 unisons: 1 string below B0, 2 below G#2 [G]. The main string carries 56 modes, the partners 16 each. Above the 16th partial the main string's modes stand for every string of the key. Bass keys stride the main string's upper modes (every 2nd to 4th partial, weighted √stride), so a low A still reaches about 7 kHz. |
| partials | `f_n = n f0 √(1+Bn²)/√(1+B)` (Fletcher 1964). B is lowest near C3 (1e-4) and rises about 3 %/semitone into the treble [G, matched to the Kawai: 2.3e-4 at C4]. |
| scale | length `0.05·2^((108−m)·0.0767)` m, capped at 1.95 [G]. Tension 700 N, rising to 1510 N in the bass [G]. Mass per length follows from f0, so string mass is physical. |
| loss | `σ_n = b1 + b3 ω_n²` (Chaigne & Askenfelt 1994), fitted through the fundamental's T60 `30·2^(−(m−21)/26)` s and HF DEC at 4 kHz [G, fitted to the Kawai]. |
| hammer | Mass, stiffness and exponent interpolate Chaigne & Askenfelt's C2/C4/C7 values: 4.9/3.0/2.2 g; K 1e9/4.5e9/1e11; p 2.3/2.5/3.0. Hysteresis α = 0.6·FELT s/m. Speed is 0.25 m/s at velocity 0 to 6 m/s at 127. |
| strike point | 1/8 of the string in the bass to 1/12.5 at the top [G]. The unison strings meet the hammer ±3 % apart, which fills the strike-point notches. |
| bridge / board | 12 plate modes, 58–985 Hz, ζ 1.6–3.4 % [G]. Each key's bridge point lies on a diagonal across the board. A resistive dense-mode term (2e-4·COUPLE s/kg, high-passed at 220 Hz) is what lets the strings lose energy at a realistic rate and gives the 6 dB/oct radiated rise. The shared bridge couples the unisons: in-phase motion drains fast, out-of-phase motion lingers (Weinreich 1977). |
| output | Board acceleration seen from two microphone points. The dense-mode part pans by key (bass left, treble right). |
| dampers | A released key adds decay to each mode by how far the damper point (strike + 3 %) moves in it. RELEASE sets the fundamental's T60. Keys above E7 (MIDI 88) have no damper. PEDAL lifts all dampers live, for held and released notes, re-blended every 32 samples. |

**Measured against the CC0 VCSL Kawai grand** (`v3` layer against
velocity 0.7, partials relative to the fundamental over 0.05–0.35 s,
analysis scripts kept in the session scratch):

| | Kawai C4 | Grand C4 | Kawai C5 | Grand C5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | 3.0e-4 | 2.3e-4 | 3.1e-4 | 4.8e-4 |
| p2..p6 dB | −2 −19 −13 −9 −26 | 6 −9 −7 −18 −18 | −18 −18 −22 −39 −30 | −7 −20 −24 −29 −29 |
| T60 fundamental | 9.5 s | 5.2 s | 9.7 s | 8.0 s |
| spectral centroid, first 100 ms | 888 Hz | 1238 Hz | 961 Hz | 1384 Hz |

Velocity runs from a 550 Hz centroid at velocity 0.1 to 2.2 kHz at 1.0,
over 26 dB of level. Against the Kawai, the model's C4 is brighter in its
2nd to 4th partials and decays about half as long.

**Cost** (ReleaseFast): about 200 ns per ringing voice, 735 ns for the
bench chord. The render word has more stores than lane mode can stash,
so the 16 voices render scalar ("renders without NEON lanes"). Note-on is
a `call:` composition of seven stages, each under the compiler's
pending-store limit.

**Not modeled:** sympathetic resonance between keys (each voice has its
own board), the duplex scale, longitudinal "phantom" partials, the
second polarization, and key and pedal noise.

## Skinwork Kit

Twelve voices, `note-pitch`, on the GM map. Notes outside 35–59 fold
into it by octave.

| note | piece | body | striker |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35, 36 | kick | membrane, 52 Hz, a 0.229 m head | felt beater, 35 g, p 2.3 |
| 37, 39 | side stick | snare head struck at the rim, light wires | stick across the rim |
| 38, 40 | snare | membrane, 185 Hz, 0.17 m, with wires | hickory stick, 12 g, Hertz p 1.5 |
| 41, 43 | floor tom | membrane, 82 Hz | stick |
| 45, 47 | mid tom | membrane, 118 Hz | stick |
| 48, 50 | high tom | membrane, 165 Hz | stick |
| 42 / 44 / 46 | closed / pedal / open hat | plate, 420 Hz | stick; closed and pedal choke open |
| 49, 52, 55, 57 | crash | plate, 190 Hz | stick |
| 51, 59 | ride | plate, 240 Hz | stick |
| 53 | ride bell | plate, 560 Hz | stick |

The lower note of each tom pair, and the second kick, sit a little lower
(×0.86). Every size, mass, tuning and T60 is an estimate of a typical kit
[G].

- **Heads** use the Bessel ratios, compressed slightly (ratio^0.94) as
  air loading does to a real drum. They are struck at a radius where
  `J_m(j_mn r)` sets each mode's drive. Loss is `σ = s0 + s1 f²` through
  two T60s.
- **Tension modulation.** The gain is `G = 150/c²` per ply (c is the
  head's wave speed). That follows from the Mylar's elastic stiffness
  against its tension: at 1 mm of excursion a snare head runs 2–3 %
  sharp. GLIDE scales it.
- **Strikers.** A hickory stick is a Hertz contact, `K = 4/3 E* √R`:
  4.4e8 on Mylar, 1.2e9 on bronze. The kick beater is felt. The stick's
  impact force, high-passed at about 1 kHz, adds the click.
- **Snare wires.** A 4 g lumped mass on a 100 Hz spring, resting a hair
  under the head center. WIRES sets the gap. Collisions drive eight wide
  buzz modes from 2.1 to 7.6 kHz.
- **Plates.** 40 modes on a stretched, jittered series
  `((i + 1.2)/1.2)^1.17`. Strike weights fall as ratio^−0.7. A bounded
  cubic coupling (BLOOM) moves energy between them, and a diffuse field
  takes over the upper modes' energy as band noise: 1.8–4 kHz high-pass,
  6.5 kHz two-pole low-pass.

**Cost:** about 530 ns per sample in Debug for the bench hit sequence.
The render word, like the piano's, renders without NEON lanes.

## Presets

- `grand`: default, bright-pop, felt-ballad, honky-tonk, sustain-wash,
  dry-studio.
- `kit`: default, jazz, rock, funk-dry, gong-wash.

## Next

- **Fretwire and Rhodes.** Fretwire could take `contact-solve` for the
  pluck and fret buzz, and moving-bridge coupling for its two-stage decay.
  Rhodes could become a struck tine-and-tonebar mode pair with an
  implicit hammer.
- **Lane mode.** Splitting the render into a lane-eligible word, or
  raising the stash limit, would roughly halve the cost.
- **Sympathetic strings.** These need a shared board across voices: a
  host-side bus or a single-voice piano.
